\section{Setup}
We consider a two-dimensional model with imaginary time $\tau=\i t$, some velocity $v$ and the euclidean coordinate $\textbf{r}=(x,y)$ with $y=v\tau$.
The fixed point action is
\begin{align}
S_0 &= \frac{1}{2\pi Kv}\intx{0}{\beta}{ \intx{-L/2}{L/2}{\klb*{\frac{1}{v}\kla*{\partial_\tau\phi}^2 + v\kla*{\partial_x\phi}^2}}{x} }{\tau} = \frac{1}{2\pi K} \int \kla*{\nabla\phi}^2 \,\d^2r.
\end{align}
We consider a general sine-Gordon perturbation
\begin{align}
S_b &= \frac{g}{\alpha^2} \sum_{s=\pm 1} \int \powe{\i bs\phi}\,\d^2r.
\end{align}
The cumulant expansion reads
\begin{align}
\log\bra*{\powe{-S_b}}_\f &= -\bra*{S_b}_\f + \frac{1}{2} \bra*{S_b^2}_\f^\c + \dots
\end{align}
and evidently requires computing ``fast'' averages.
Following the prescription
\begin{align}
\int_\f &\rightarrow \int_\textbf{k} \klb*{f\kla*{\frac{k^2}{\Lambda^2}} - f\kla*{\frac{k^2}{\Lambda^2 \powe{-2\d l}}}} = -2\frac{\d l}{\Lambda^2} \int_\textbf{k} k^2 f'\kla*{\frac{k^2}{\Lambda^2}},
\end{align}
where $f(x)$ determines the form of the cut-off.
For example, $f(x)=\Theta(1-x)$ gives a hard cut-off in momentum space, while $f(x)=\powe{-x}$ leads to gaussian integrals and strikes a balance between locality in both real and momentum space.

To compute the averages, we make use of the remarkable identity
\begin{align}
\bra*{\prod \powe{C_j}} &= \powe{\frac{1}{2}\bra*{\kla*{\sum C_j}^2}} \powe{\frac{1}{2} \sum_{j<k} \klb*{C_j,C_k}} = \powe{\frac{1}{2} \sum \bra*{C_j^2}} \powe{\sum_{j<k} \bra*{C_jC_k}}.
\end{align}
The Fourier representation of the fields reads
\begin{align}
\phi(\textbf{r}) &= \frac{1}{\beta L} \sum_\textbf{q} \powe{\i\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{q}} \phi(\textbf{q}),
\end{align}
where $\textbf{q}=(\omega_n^\B/v, k)$ and $\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{q} = \omega_n^\B \tau - kx$ with $\omega_n^\B = \frac{2\pi n}{\beta}$ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.
The action then takes the form
\begin{align}
S_0 &= \frac{1}{2\pi K \beta^2 L^2} \int \sum_{\textbf{q},\textbf{q}'} \powe{\i \textbf{r} \cdot \kla*{\textbf{q} + \textbf{q}'}} \i\textbf{q}\phi(\textbf{q}) \cdot \i\textbf{q}' \phi(\textbf{q}') = \frac{v}{2\pi K\beta L} \sum_\textbf{q} q^2 \abs*{\phi(\textbf{q})}^2.
\end{align}
Field integration then yields $\bra*{\phi(\textbf{q}_1) \phi(\textbf{q}_2)} = \delta_{\textbf{q}_1,-\textbf{q}_2} \frac{\pi K\beta L}{vq_1^2}$, and therefore
\begin{align}
\Phi_\f(\textbf{r}) &= \bra*{\phi_\f(\textbf{r})\phi_\f(0)}_\f = \frac{1}{\beta^2L^2} \sum_{\f,\f'} \powe{\i\textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} \bra*{\phi_\f(\textbf{q})\phi_\f(\textbf{q}')}_\f = \frac{\pi K}{v\beta L} \sum_\f \powe{\i\textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} \frac{1}{q^2}.
\end{align}
Taking the limit $L,\beta\rightarrow\infty$, we can rewrite this as
\begin{align}
\Phi_\f(\textbf{r}) &= \frac{\pi K}{v\beta L} \frac{L}{2\pi} \frac{\beta v}{2\pi} \int_\f \powe{\i\textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} \frac{1}{q^2} = \frac{K\d l}{2} \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r}),
\end{align}
where we defined
\begin{align}
\tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r}) &= -\frac{1}{\pi\Lambda^2} \intrnd{2}{\powe{\i\textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} f'\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}}}{q} = -\frac{1}{\pi \Lambda^2} \intx{0}{\infty}{ \intx{0}{2\pi}{ \powe{\i rq\cos\theta} f'\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}}q }{\theta} }{q} \\
&= -\frac{1}{\Lambda^2}  \intx{0}{\infty}{f'\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}} J_0(rq)}{q^2} = -\intx{0}{\infty}{ f'(x) J_0\kla*{\Lambda r \sqrt{x}} }{x}.
\end{align}
Thus, independent from the choice of the cut-off function, the fast correlator only depends on the magnitude of $\textbf{r}$.

There are now two important observations.
First, the cumulant expansion will require a rapidly decaying $\tilde{\Phi}(\Lambda r) \equiv \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})$, and thus a sharp cut-off generally leads to technical problems.
Second, the quantitative results will depend on the precise choice of the cut-off function.
To better control the latter part, we know simply \emph{assume} that the first property is satisfied (even if it is not, we return to this later), and then try to compute the RG equations without explicitly choosing $f(x)$.

To understand how this can be possible, note that typically the expansion leads to integrals of the form
\begin{align}
I(n,m) &= \int x^{2n}y^{2m} \tilde{\Phi}(r)\,\d^2r.
\end{align}
Due to the symmetry between $x$ and $y$, only even powers give non-zero results.

There are two approaches to evaluating $I(n,m)$.
First, using the representation with the Bessel function $J_0$, we have
\begin{align}
I(n,m) &= -\intx{0}{\infty}{ f'(t) \intrnd{2}{x^{2n} y^{2m} J_0(r \sqrt{t})}{r} }{t}\\
&= -\intx{0}{\infty}{f'(t) \intx{0}{\infty}{r^{2n+2m+1} J_0(r\sqrt{t}) \intx{0}{2\pi}{\cos(\theta)^{2n} \sin(\theta)^{2m}}{\theta} }{r} }{t}\\
&= -2 B\kla*{n+\frac{1}{2}, m+\frac{1}{2}} \intx{0}{\infty}{ f'(t) t^{-n-m-1} }{t} \intx{0}{\infty}{u^{2n+2m+1} J_0(u)}{u}.
\end{align}
The moments of the Bessel functions are given by \cite{NIST_Bessel}
\begin{align}
\intx{0}{\infty}{u^\mu J_\nu(u)}{u} &= 2^\mu \frac{\Gamma\kla*{\frac{\nu+\mu+1}{2}}}{\Gamma\kla*{\frac{\nu-\mu+1}{2}}} \label{eqn:J0_moments},\\
\intx{0}{\infty}{u^{\nu+1} J_\nu(bu) \powe{-au^2}}{u} &= \frac{b^\nu }{(2a)^{\nu+1}}\powe{-\frac{b^2}{4a}}.
\end{align}
The Bessel functions themself admit the series representation
\begin{align}
J_\nu(z) &= \kla*{\frac{z}{2}}^\nu \sum_{k\ge 0} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!} \frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu+k+1)} \kla*{\frac{z}{2}}^{2k}.
\end{align}
\autoref{eqn:J0_moments} only holds for $\mu < \frac{1}{2}$, so we can not directly make use of this in our integral.
Instead, inserting the series, we find
\begin{align}
I(n,m) &= -2B\kla*{n+\frac{1}{2}, m+\frac{1}{2}} \intx{0}{\infty}{f'(t) \intx{0}{\infty}{r^{2n+2m+1}  \sum_{k\ge 0} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2} \kla*{\frac{r\sqrt{t}}{2}}^{2k} }{r} }{t}\\
&= -2B\kla*{n+\frac{1}{2}, m+\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k\ge 0} 2^{-2k} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2} \intx{0}{\infty}{f'(t) t^k \intx{0}{R}{r^{2n+2m+2k+1} }{r} }{t} \\
&= -2\frac{\Gamma\kla*{n+\frac{1}{2}} \Gamma\kla*{m+\frac{1}{2}}}{(n+m)!}\sum_{k\ge 0} 2^{-2k} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2} \frac{R^{2n+2m+2k+2}}{2n+2m+2k+2} \intx{0}{\infty}{f'(t) t^k }{t}.
\end{align}
This calculation is plagued by convergence problems.
A quite different approach is to instead write
\begin{align}
I(n,m) &= -\frac{1}{\pi\Lambda^2} \intrnd{2}{ f'\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}} \intrnd{2}{\powe{\i \textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} x^{2n} y^{2m} }{r} }{q}\\
&= -\frac{1}{\pi\Lambda^2} \intrnd{2}{ f'\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}} \intrnd{2}{ (-\i \partial_{q_x})^{2n} (-\i \partial_{q_y})^{2m} \powe{\i xq_x+\i yq_y} }{r} }{q}\\
&= -\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda^2} (-1)^{n+m} \intrnd{2}{ f'\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}} \partial_{q_x}^{2n} \delta(q_x) \partial_{q_y}^{2m} \delta(q_y) }{q}\\
&= -\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda^2} (-1)^{n+m} \partial_{q_x}^{2n}\partial_{q_y}^{2m} f'\kla[\bigg]{\frac{q_x^2+q_y^2}{\Lambda^2}} \bigg\vert_{\textbf{q}=0}.
\end{align}

\section{Including normal-ordering}
Giamarchi claims that it is crucial to normal-order the exponential of the slow fields before expanding.
This means we effectively have to include the factor
\begin{align}
\powe{\i sb\klb*{\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1) - \phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}} &= \normord{\powe{\i sb\klb*{\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1) - \phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}}} \bra*{\powe{\i bs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i bs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}}_\s = \normord{(\dots)} \powe{b^2\Phi_\s(\textbf{r})},
\end{align}
where $\Phi_\s(\textbf{r}) = \bra*{\phi_\s(\textbf{r})\phi_\s(0) - \phi_\s(0)^2}_\s$.
The slow correlator is given by
\begin{align}
\Phi_\s(\textbf{r}) &= \frac{K}{4\pi} \int_\s \kla*{\powe{\i\textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} - 1}\frac{1}{q^2} = \frac{K}{4\pi} \intrnd{2}{ f\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}} \frac{\powe{\i \textbf{r}\cdot \textbf{q}} - 1}{q^2} }{q}\\ &= \frac{K}{4\pi} \intx{0}{\infty}{ f\kla*{\frac{q^2}{\Lambda^2}} \frac{1}{q} \intx{0}{2\pi}{ \kla*{\powe{\i qr\cos\vartheta} - 1} }{\vartheta} }{q} = \frac{K}{4} \intx{0}{\infty}{ f(u) \frac{J_0\kla*{\Lambda r\sqrt{u}} - 1}{u} }{u}.
\end{align}
The moments that occur in the RG calculation are then generically
\begin{align}
I(n,m) &= \int x^{2n} y^{2m} \tilde{\Phi}(r) \powe{b^2\Phi_\s(\textbf{r})} = 2\frac{B\kla*{n+\frac{1}{2}, m+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Lambda^{2n+2m+2}} C_{2n+2m+1},
\end{align}
where the coefficients are defined by
\begin{align}
C_n &= -\intx{0}{\infty}{\intx{0}{\infty}{f'(t) J_0\kla{s\sqrt{t}} s^n \powe{b^2 \frac{K}{4} \intx{0}{\infty}{ f(u) \frac{1}{u} \klb*{J_0\kla*{s\sqrt{u}} - 1} }{u}}}{s}}{t}.
\end{align}
If the series converges, we can solve the integral in the exponent via
\begin{align}
\intx{0}{\infty}{ f(u) \frac{1}{u} \klb*{J_0\kla*{s\sqrt{u}} - 1} }{u} &= \intx{0}{\infty}{ f(u) \frac{1}{u} \sum_{k\ge 1} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2} \kla*{\frac{us^2}{4}}^k }{u} \\
&= \sum_{k\ge 1} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2} \frac{s^{2k}}{2^{2k}} \intx{0}{\infty}{f(u)u^{k-1}}{u} = \sum_{k\ge 1} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2} \frac{s^{2k}}{2^{2k}} \mathcal{M}\klc*{f}(k),
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{M}\klc*{f}(s) = \intx{0}{\infty}{u^{s-1} f(u)}{u}$ is the Mellin transform.
\subsection{Sharp cut-off}
Let $f(x)=\Theta(1-x)$.
Then the Mellin transform is $\mathcal{M}\klc*{f}(s) = \intx{0}{1}{u^{s-1}}{u} = \frac{1}{s}$.

\end{align}

\section{RG Flow Equations}
In this section, we compute the RG order by order via the cumulant expansion.
We only consider a single sine-Gordon perturbation with coupling $g$, and thus the beta function is
\begin{align}
\frac{\d g}{\d l} &= \beta_g(g,K) = \beta_g^{(0)} + \beta_g^{(1)},\\
\frac{\d K}{\d l} &= \beta_K(g,K) = \beta_K^{(0)} + \beta_K^{(1)} + \dots.
\end{align}
\subsection{Tree-level}
The tree-level term is given by
\begin{align}
\bra*{S_b}_\f &= \frac{g}{\alpha^2} \sum_s \int \powe{\i bs\phi_\s} \bra*{\powe{\i bs\phi_\f}}_\f = \frac{g}{\alpha^2} \sum_s \int \powe{\i bs\phi_\s} \powe{-\frac{b^2}{2} \Phi_\f(0)} = \frac{g'}{g} S_{b,\s},
\end{align}
where $g' = g\powe{-b^2\frac{K}{4} \d l}$ since $\tilde{\Phi}(0)=1$ regardless of the cut-off function.
In $d=2$ there is no field renormalization (the fields must be dimensionless for $\cos(\phi)$ to make sense), and rescaling the variables gives a factor of $s=\powe{\d l}$ for every spatial variable (and $\frac{1}{s}$ for every derivative).
The effective action is $S_\text{eff}^{(0)} = S + \bra*{S_b}_\f$ and thus
\begin{align}
\beta_g^{(0)} &= \kla*{2 - b^2 \frac{K}{4}} g.
\end{align}
\subsection{One-loop corrections}
The one-loop corrections are determined by
\begin{align}
\bra*{S_b^2}_\f^\c &= \frac{g^2}{\alpha^4} \sum_{s,s'} \int \powe{\i bs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i bs'\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)} \bra*{\powe{\i bs\phi_\f(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i bs' \phi_\f(\textbf{r}_2)}}_\f^\c\\
&= \frac{g^2}{\alpha^4} \sum_{s,s'} \int \powe{\i bs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i bs'\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)} \klc*{\powe{-b^2\Phi_\f(0)} \powe{-b^2ss' \Phi_\f(\textbf{r})} - \powe{-b^2 \Phi_\f(0)} }\\
&=\frac{g^2 b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2}\d l \sum_{s,s'} (-ss') \int \powe{\i bs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i bs'\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)} \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})\\
&= \frac{g^2b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2} \d l \sum_s \int \klc*{\powe{\i bs \klb*{\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1) - \phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}} - \powe{\i bs \klb*{\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1) + \phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}}} \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r}) \equiv \textbf{I} + \textbf{II}.
\end{align}
This is where the assumption of a localized $\tilde{\Phi}$ comes into play.
In order to make progress, we need to expand in terms of the relative coordinate $\textbf{r}$.
The second term then becomes
\begin{align}
\textbf{II} &\simeq -\frac{g^2b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2} \d l \sum_s \int \powe{\i 2bs \phi_\s(\textbf{R})} \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r}) = \frac{2\pi K g^2 b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) \d l \frac{1}{\alpha^2} \sum_s \int \powe{\i 2bs \phi_\s},
\end{align}
because $\int \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r}) = -\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda^2} f'(0)$.
This already demonstrates one of the key features of perturbative RG: Higher order terms typically spawn an entire hierarchy of new contribution.
Although the original model only contains a single sine-Gordon term with $g\equiv g_b$, at one-loop order we generate a new term $\sim S_{2b}$.
Let us now also talk about degrees of separation.
At zero degrees of separation, we ignore any contributions to perturbations that, like $g_{2b}$, are not present in the original model.
At one degree of separation, we take into account that such terms are generated, but we then disregard their feedback into the flow equations.
This pattern continues, and after just two degrees of separation, we already have all the terms listed above.
The reason this seldom considered, is the complexity of the result combined with its relatively insignificant contribution.

The first term requires a bit more care, because we should normal-order before trying to expand the exponential.
To do this, note that
\begin{align}
\prod \powe{\i A_j \phi_j} = \normord{\prod \powe{\i A_j \phi_j}} \cdot \const,
\end{align}
where the constant is yet to be determined.
This structure immediately follows from repeatedly applying the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula and realizing that the commutators of any $\phi_j$ are number valued (since $\phi\sim \sum b_q + b_q^\dagger$).
Determining the constant is then straight-forward, because the expectation value of the normal-ordered product is 1.
Consequently,
\begin{align}
\const &= \bra*{\prod \powe{\i A_j \phi_j}} = \powe{-\frac{1}{2} \sum A_j^2 \bra*{\phi(0)^2}} \powe{-\sum_{j<k} A_jA_k \bra*{\phi(\textbf{r}_j) \phi(\textbf{r}_k)}}.
\end{align}
In our case, the fields only contain slow modes and therefore both the expectation value and the normal-ordering are also with respect to the slow modes.
For the explicit example,
\begin{align}
\textbf{I} &= \frac{g^2b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2}\d l \sum_s \int \normord{\powe{\i bs \klb*{\phi_\s\kla*{\textbf{R} + \frac{\textbf{r}}{2}} - \phi_\s\kla*{\textbf{R} - \frac{\textbf{r}}{2}}}}} \powe{b^2 \bra*{\phi_\s(\textbf{r}) \phi_\s(0) - \phi_\s(0)^2} } \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})\\
&\simeq \frac{g^2b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2}\d l \sum_s\int  \normord{\powe{\i bs \textbf{r}\cdot \nabla_\textbf{R} \phi_\s(\textbf{R})}} \powe{b^2 \Phi_\s(\textbf{r}) } \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})\\
&\simeq -\frac{g^2b^4}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2}\d l \sum_s \int \normord{\kla*{\textbf{r}\cdot \nabla_\textbf{R} \phi_\s(\textbf{R})}^2} \powe{b^2 \Phi_\s(\textbf{r}) } \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})\\
&= -\frac{g^2b^4}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2}\d l \int \normord{\kla*{\nabla_\textbf{R} \phi_\s(\textbf{R})}^2} \int x^2 \powe{b^2 \Phi_\s(\textbf{r}) } \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})\\
&= -\frac{g^2b^4}{\alpha^4} \pi K^2 \d l I(1,0) S_{0,\s}.
\end{align}
The contribution to the effective action is $-\frac{1}{2} \bra*{S_b^2}_\f$, i.e.
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{K'} &= \frac{1}{K}\kla*{1 + \frac{\pi K^2g^2b^4}{2\alpha^4} I(1,0) \d l},
\end{align}
which can be rewritten as ($\frac{1}{K+\d K} - \frac{1}{K} = -\frac{\d K}{K^2}$)
\begin{align}
\beta_K^{(1)} = -\frac{\pi K^3g^2b^4}{2\alpha^4} I(1,0).
\end{align}
The new sine-Gordon term gives
\begin{align}
\beta_{g_{2b}}^{(1)} &= -\frac{\pi K g^2b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2}.
\end{align}
Let us now also consider the general mixed terms
\begin{align}
\bra*{S_{nb} S_{mb}}_\f^\c &= \frac{g_{nb} g_{mb}}{\alpha^4} \sum_{s,s'} \int \powe{\i nbs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i mbs'\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)} \bra*{\powe{\i nbs\phi_\f(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i mbs' \phi_\f(\textbf{r}_2)}}_\f^\c\\
&= \frac{g_{nb} g_{mb}}{\alpha^4} \sum_{s,s'} \int \powe{\i nbs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i mbs'\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)} \powe{-nmb^2\Phi_\f(0)} \klc*{\powe{-nmb^2ss' \Phi_\f(\textbf{r})} - 1 }\\
&=\frac{g^2 b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2}\d l \sum_{s,s'} (-ss') \int \powe{\i nbs\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1)} \powe{\i mbs'\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)} \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r})\\
&= \frac{g_{nb} g_{mb} nm b^2}{\alpha^4} \frac{K}{2} \d l \sum_s \int \klc*{\powe{\i bs \klb*{n\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1) - m\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}} - \powe{\i bs \klb*{n\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_1) + m\phi_\s(\textbf{r}_2)}}} \tilde{\Phi}_\f(\textbf{r}).
\end{align}
Since $n\neq m$, both of these contributions are once again approximately sine-Gordon terms, i.e.
\begin{align}
\bra*{S_{nb} S_{mb}}_\f^\c &\simeq \frac{2\pi K g_{nb} g_{mb} nm b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) \d l  \klc*{\frac{S_{(n+m)b,\s}}{g_{(n+m)b}} - \frac{S_{|n-m|b,\s}}{g_{|n-m|b}}}.
\end{align}
Note that all of these mixed terms also appear with swapped $n$ and $m$, and due to the symmetry in this example this gives an extra factor of 2.
\subsection{Final result}
The full flow equations are
\begin{align}
\frac{\d K}{\d l} &= -\frac{\pi^2 K^3g_b^2b^4}{2(\Lambda\alpha)^4} C_3 -\frac{\pi^2 K^3g_{2b}^2(2b)^4}{2(\Lambda\alpha)^4} C_3 -\frac{\pi^2 K^3g_{3b}^2(3b)^4}{2(\Lambda\alpha)^4} C_3 -\frac{\pi^2 K^3g_{4b}^2(4b)^4}{2(\Lambda\alpha)^4} C_3 + \dots,\\
\frac{\d g_b}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - b^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_b + 2\frac{\pi K g_{b} g_{2b} 2b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + 2\frac{\pi K g_{2b} g_{3b} 6b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + 2\frac{\pi K g_{3b} g_{4b} 12b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + \dots,\\
\frac{\d g_{2b}}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - (2b)^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_{2b} -\frac{\pi K g_b^2b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + 2\frac{\pi K g_b g_{3b} 3b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + 2\frac{\pi K g_{2b} g_{4b} 8b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + \dots,\\
\frac{\d g_{3b}}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - (3b)^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_{3b} - 2\frac{\pi K g_b g_{2b} 2b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + 2\frac{\pi K g_b g_{4b} 4b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + \dots,\\
\frac{\d g_{4b}}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - (4b)^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_{4b} -\frac{\pi K g_{2b}^2(2b)^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) - 2\frac{\pi Kg_b g_{3b} 3b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) + \dots,\\
&\vdots
\end{align}
with initial conditions $K=K_0$, $g_b=g$ and $g_{2b}= g_{4b} = \dots =0$.
Clearly, despite starting with only a single coupling, we eventually have to consider infinitely many perturbations.
However, unless the new terms are sufficiently interesting (e.g. generate new physics), it is important to note that they are less and less relevant.
In this particular example, already $g_{2b}$ has a scaling dimension of $b^2K$, which for $b=4$ renders this irrelevant for any $K>\frac{1}{8}$ -- and Luttinger parameters typically do not take such extreme values.

Nevertheless, it is insightful to analyse the patterns that emerge from these ``self-couplings''.
Summing up all contributions, we have
\begin{align}
\frac{\d K}{\d l} &= -\frac{\pi^2K^3b^4}{2(\Lambda\alpha)^2} C_3 \sum_{n\ge 1} g_{nb}^2n^2,\\
\frac{\d g_b}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - b^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_{b} + \frac{2\pi Kb^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) \sum_{n\ge 1} g_{nb} g_{(n+1)b} n(n+1),\\
\frac{\d g_{2b}}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - (2b)^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_{2b} + \frac{\pi K b^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) \klc[\bigg]{-g_b^2 + 2 \sum_{n\ge 1} g_{nb} g_{(n+2)b} n(n+2)},\\
&\vdots\\
\frac{\d g_{mb}}{\d l} &= \kla*{2 - (mb)^2 \frac{K}{4}} g_{mb} + \frac{\pi Kb^2}{(\Lambda\alpha)^2} f'(0) \sum_{n,n'\ge 1} g_{nb} g_{n'b} nn' \klc*{ \delta_{|n-n'|,m} - \delta_{n+n',m} },
\end{align} %\klc[\bigg]{-\sum_{n,n'\in I_m} (2 - \delta_{n,n'}) g_{nb} g_{n'b} nn' + 2\sum_{n\ge 1} g_{nb} g_{(n+m)b} n(n+m) }
where $I_m = \klc*{(n,n')\in \mathbb{N}^2\,\vert\,n+n'=m}$.
The expression in the final parentheses can be simplified to
\begin{align}
2\sum_{n> n'\ge 1} g_{nb} g_{n'b} nn' \klc*{\delta_{n-n',m} - \delta_{n+n',m}} - g_{mb/2}^2 (m/2)^2,
\end{align}
although the single term only exists for $m$ even.

Due to the explicit $f'(0)$, this approach only works for sufficiently smooth cut-off functions.
In particular, the sharp cut-off $f(x)=\Theta(1-x)$ once again turns out to be problematic.
Therefore, we now limit ourselves to $f(x)=\powe{-x}$.
Surprisingly, the influence of higher order terms is much more important than initially assumed.
In particular, for $b=4$ the RG flow remains stable even for $K\le \frac{1}{2}$ -- despite one would TODO.
